How to choose a rolling cart for a small kitchen

  • Measure the floor space where the cart will sit, with any drop leaf both open and closed
  • Leave enough room to walk past it and open the oven, dishwasher and fridge
  • Check the cart height against your counters
  • Look for lockable wheels so it does not roll while you work
  • Check the weight capacity for the appliances and items you plan to put on top
  • Think about your floor, since some wheels can mark soft floors

Frequently asked questions

Is a rolling cart or a fixed island better for a small kitchen?

A rolling cart is usually the better fit for a small kitchen or rental, because you can move it or fold the leaf away when you need floor space.

How much room do I need around a kitchen cart?

Leave enough space to walk past and open the oven, dishwasher and fridge doors. Measure with the drop leaf open before you buy.

Do kitchen carts need assembly?

Most do. Check the product page and recent buyer reviews for assembly details, and plan some time and a second pair of hands for larger carts.
