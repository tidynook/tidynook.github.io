How to choose a stackable food storage set

  • Measure the shelf height and depth where the set will be stored, and compare it with how the set stacks
  • Check that the lids fit more than one container, so you do not end up with spare lids
  • Look at the sizes in the set and match them with what you store, such as leftovers, meal prep or dry goods
  • Check the microwave, dishwasher and freezer details on each listing
  • Choose between lightweight plastic and heavier glass, based on how you use the containers
  • Keep lids stored together, so you can find the matching one

Frequently asked questions

Is glass or plastic better for food storage containers?

It depends on how you use them. Plastic is lighter and easier to carry, while glass is heavier and can suit reheating if the listing allows it. Check the heat and lid details on the product page.

How do I stop containers and lids from taking over a cabinet?

Choose a set with lids that fit more than one container, measure your shelf, and keep the lids stored together.

Are stackable containers good for small kitchens?

Often yes, because they stack and nest. Measure your shelf height and check how the set stacks before you order.
